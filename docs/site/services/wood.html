Wood provision

Method as implemented, and the code of each step

This page describes the method of the wood provision accounts as the tool implements it. Each step points to the stage of the tool that carries it out, and the code annex at the end describes each stage with links to its code. The numbers and tables that a run produces are in the report of that run, in outputs/wood/report/, which carries the same method text together with them.

1 Methods

1.1 Overview

The accounts deliver four groups of quantities for every reporting year. The actual flow is the net annual increment (NAI) of the forest available for wood supply. The sustainable flow is the part of the wood removed from that forest that stays within the increment, and overuse is the part that exceeds it. Each of these is reported in cubic metres and in euro, in current prices and in constant prices of 2010. The quantities come in two forms, as tables by country (NUTS0) and by NUTS2 and NUTS3 region in an Excel workbook, and as maps on a grid of 100 m cells. The section on products at the end describes both.

Forest statistics report these quantities by country, and they do not say where in the country the wood grows. The work therefore runs in four steps, which the sections below follow in order.

  1. Eurostat statistics give the NAI and the removals of each country and year, and the gaps in them are filled by rules that the section on the actual flow explains.
  2. Comparing removals with the NAI gives the sustainable flow and the overuse of each country.
  3. A unit value in euro per cubic metre, built from timber and export prices, turns the physical quantities into values.
  4. A spatial key, which combines where forest grows with how productive the vegetation is, shares each national value among the regions and the 100 m cells of the country. Regions and cells therefore add up to the national values.

The accounts cover the EU27 with the NUTS 2024 boundaries, at the country level, NUTS2 and NUTS3 (Eurostat GISCO, 2024). See the stage prepare in the code annex. The boundaries carry the notice © EuroGeographics for the administrative boundaries. All grids use the projection EPSG:3035, in which every cell covers the same area on the ground. Malta has no forest available for wood supply and no forestry activity (Vallecillo et al., 2019), so its values are zero throughout.

1.2 Actual flow

The source of the actual flow is the table for_vol_efa of Eurostat, which collects the European Forest Accounts in thousand cubic metres over bark (Eurostat, 2026e). It holds the NAI and the removals of each country, both for the forest available for wood supply (FAWS) and for total forest (FOR) (see the stage accounts in the code annex). It needs completing, since few Member States report the NAI of forest available for wood supply in the early years.

1.2.1 Filling the gaps

INCA Part III fills the gaps with the estimates of the carbon budget model of the JRC, which simulates the growth of the forests of each country from their inventories (La Notte et al., 2021; Pilli & Grassi, 2021). These estimates are published in a workbook that combines the model with the forest accounts and gives the total NAI of each country from 2000 to 2019, with the values after 2016 held at their level of 2015 (see the stage prepare in the code annex). The series refers to total forest, and the workbook also gives the share of available forest in the growing stock, so two versions of the fill are possible. The INCA tool uses a third, the NAI of total forest reported to Eurostat. Each run compares all three with the values by Member State printed in the annexes of Part III, which are the published reference for the method (see the stage accounts in the code annex).

The total forest estimate of the carbon budget model follows the method that Part III states and reproduces its Member State values best, so a decision was made to use it. The NAI is therefore taken in this order. A value reported for available forest comes first. The carbon budget estimate comes next. Any remaining gap takes the mean of the nearest earlier and later values of the same country, and gaps at the end of the series repeat the last value.

The carbon budget estimate covers total forest and fills a statistic of available forest, so it probably overstates the flow in the countries that take it. Each run measures this effect against the EU27 aggregates that Eurostat publishes, which it estimates itself.

Eurostat holds few values for the last reporting year, and the method says nothing about how far a value may be carried. A decision was made to carry the last value, since the increment changes slowly and any trend applied to it would add an assumption that the data cannot support.

1.2.2 Changes of source within the series

A Member State that starts reporting available forest moves from the estimate of the model to its own figure, and the two can differ widely. We kept the series as the method builds it and did not adjust the model estimates to the reported levels, so changes over time within these countries should be read together with the list of these changes in the report of each run.

1.3 Sustainable flow and overuse

The accounting reports class wood among the services with a regeneration rate that use can exceed (La Notte et al., 2021), and the handbook of ecosystem accounts describes overharvesting as removals above the NAI (Eurostat, 2026a). Neither gives a formula for wood, so we adapted the one of Part III for water purification, in which overuse is the difference between the actual and the sustainable flow and the threshold defines the sustainable flow. Regeneration is the threshold for wood, which gives these two equations for each country and year.

\[ \text{Overuse} = \max(\text{Removals} - \text{NAI},\ 0) \tag{1}\]

\[ \text{Sustainable flow} = \min(\text{Removals},\ \text{NAI}) \tag{2}\]

Removals are the removals of available forest in for_vol_efa, which Member States report more widely than the NAI. They take the same rule for gaps, with reported removals of total forest in place of the model estimate. The two quantities add up to the removals in every country and year (see the stage accounts in the code annex). A single year above the threshold can be a temporary peak, for example after a storm, and the handbook treats overharvesting as a matter of an extended period, so the results also show the ratio of removals to NAI of every country and year.

1.4 Monetary valuation

The value is the physical quantity times a unit value in euro per cubic metre. INCA Part III derives the unit value in three steps (La Notte et al., 2021). The output of forest trees in for_sup_cp (Eurostat, 2026d) divided by the NAI gives the unit value of the NAI, which few Member States report in the early years. The roundwood exports of for_basic (Eurostat, 2026c), in value and in volume, give a unit value for most countries and years, but this price includes the harvesting and transport added on the way to the market. The ratio between the two then carries the export price back to the standing tree.

\[ \text{Ratio}_{c,t} = \frac{\text{Unit value of exports}_{c,t}}{\text{Unit value of NAI}_{c,t}} \tag{3}\]

\[ \text{Completed unit value}_{c,t} = \frac{\text{Unit value of exports}_{c,t}}{\widehat{\text{Ratio}}_{c,t}} \tag{4}\]

Where the ratio is missing, \(\widehat{\text{Ratio}}\) takes the mean ratio of the country, or the EU mean if the country has no ratio at all (see the stage accounts in the code annex). Gaps in the export price take the same means. Means run over 2000 to 2023 for a country. The EU mean takes the mean of the countries in each year and then the mean of those years from 2012 to 2023, where coverage is better, as in the INCA tool.

1.4.1 Screening the ratio

The ratio is very sensitive to small denominators. An output of forest trees recorded close to zero yields a very large ratio, which drives up the EU mean, so every Member State that takes the EU mean would end up with a unit value close to zero.

The INCA tool removes all the timber values of Croatia, Finland, Greece, Portugal and Sweden and the export points of Denmark in 2014 and Greece in 2006 (European Union, 2026), and we did the same. The data used here are more recent than that list, so it does not catch everything. A decision was made to extend the rule in a way that the data support. A Member State whose median ratio falls below 1 or above 100 loses all its timber values, and any single observation outside that range is dropped. The lower bound follows from the meaning of the ratio, since a standing tree cannot be worth more than the harvested roundwood.

Export prices have their own errors. A decision was made to drop a point that lies more than three times above or below the median of its country, a rule that also catches the Greek point of the INCA tool.

1.4.2 Constant prices

Part III moves its other accounts to constant prices, so that the accounts follow changes in the service and leave aside changes in the market (La Notte et al., 2021). It does not state a deflator or a price basis for wood. The accounts therefore report both current and constant prices. Constant prices multiply the value of each year by a deflator, which is the HICP of the EU27 (Eurostat, 2026b) rebased to 2010. A single EU27 index and the base year 2010 are a method choice that keeps all the accounts of this series on one price basis, and the INCA tool uses national indices with base year 2000 instead.

Overuse and the sustainable flow take the same unit value as the NAI of their country and year, since the method has no separate price for them.

1.5 The spatial key

Part II spreads national flows over the forest of Corine Land Cover in proportion to Dry Matter Productivity (Vallecillo et al., 2019), and the Earth observation roadmap of the INCA project keeps a forest mask and that proxy (Zurbarán Nucci et al., 2025). The weight of a 100 m cell in year \(t\) is therefore

\[ w_{i,t} = F_{i,t} \cdot P_{i,t} \tag{5}\]

where \(F_{i,t}\) is 1 for forest in the Corine accounting layer of the year (European Environment Agency, 2026) and 0 elsewhere, and \(P_{i,t}\) is the annual productivity resampled to 100 m with bilinear interpolation (see the stage prepare in the code annex). A cell then receives

\[ \text{Value}_{i,t} = \text{Value}_{c,t} \cdot \frac{w_{i,t}}{\sum_{j \in c} w_{j,t}} \tag{6}\]

so the cells of a country add up to its national value. The maps use these 100 m weights directly. The regional tables use the same weights added up to 1 km, as the section on regional tables explains (see the stage maps_100m in the code annex).

Forest mask. Part II limits the mask to the broad-leaved, coniferous and mixed forest classes (311, 312 and 313) and leaves out transitional woodland and shrub (324) (Vallecillo et al., 2019). The INCA tool includes that class, so the two differ, and a decision was made to follow the report.

Availability. The mask holds all forest, available or not. Part II tried to delineate available forest through slope and protected areas and rejected this, because thresholds differ across countries and the result can mislead (Vallecillo et al., 2019). A map of available forest now exists for 2020 (European Commission, Joint Research Centre, 2026), and a decision was made to leave it out. It covers one year in a series of five, the reports do not call for it, and the statistic that is spread already refers to available forest.

Productivity. Part II and the roadmap name Dry Matter Productivity. This work uses Gross Dry Matter Productivity, a separate Copernicus product that also counts the autotrophic respiration of the plants, because it was the product at hand and the other needs a further bulk download.1 The proxy only shares a national value among the cells of a country, so what matters is the relative pattern within a country and national totals do not depend on it. The 1 km product, version 2, serves 2000, 2006 and 2012 (Copernicus Land Monitoring Service, n.d.-c), and the 300 m product, version 2, serves 2018 and 2024 (Copernicus Land Monitoring Service, n.d.-d).

A test then measured what this choice costs (see the stage prepare in the code annex). It compared the Dry Matter Productivity products (Copernicus Land Monitoring Service, n.d.-a, n.d.-b) with half of the gross products, since the quality page of the 1 km product describes a constant fraction of 0.5. It covers 10 of the 180 pairs of files behind the key, namely the files ending on 10 January and 10 July of each reporting year, over the whole of Europe. The tolerance was set before the comparison. In every pair, the absolute difference could reach 1.0 percent of the productivity total, and the cells with data in only one product could reach 0.1 percent of the cells. Dry Matter Productivity equals exactly half of the gross value in every cell, with data in the same cells. A constant ratio leaves the share of every cell unchanged, so on this evidence the two products give the same key, and the key stays on Gross Dry Matter Productivity. The other 170 pairs were not compared, so the finding rests on this sample.

1.6 Regional tables

The value of a region is the national value times the share of the region in the weight of its country in that year, so the regions add up to the country and follow the same key as the maps (see the stages prepare and accounts in the code annex). Shares are computed on the 1 km weights, the sum of the 100 m weights in each 1 km cell. All regions of a country therefore share one unit value, and the overuse of a country reaches all its regions in proportion to their weight, since the data do not show where removals take place inside a country.

1.7 Products

Workbook. The workbook holds one sheet for each group of quantities and NUTS level. The sheets of the actual flow give the NAI in thousand cubic metres, and those of overuse give the sustainable flow and the overuse side by side. The monetary sheets give million euro in current prices and in constant prices of 2010. The reporting years are the columns, and a first sheet explains the contents.

Maps. There are six maps for each reporting year on the 100 m grid, built with the 100 m weights of Equation 5. See the stage maps_100m in the code annex. They hold the actual flow, the sustainable flow and the overuse, each in physical terms and in constant prices of 2010, which gives 30 maps. Each cell holds a whole number in small units, cubic decimetres of wood or euro cents. Whole numbers make the files several times smaller than decimals, and every country keeps its total to within one part in a thousand. The files are Cloud Optimised GeoTIFF without overviews, which keeps them small, and a GIS such as QGIS draws them quickly as they are.

Working layers. The run also spreads the actual flow and its value over the 1 km weights. These 1 km layers are not a product of the accounts. They give the map figures of the report, drawn at 10 km, and a check of the 1 km weights against the national values. See the stage maps_1km in the code annex.

1.8 Checks

The run stops when a check fails and records the result. The 100 m maps are added up by country from the whole numbers as they are written, and compared with the national values country by country. See the stage maps_100m in the code annex. The 1 km layers are read back from disk and compared in the same way. The run also stops if the NUTS2 and NUTS3 totals differ from the NUTS0 totals in any table, metric or year.

Code annex

The stages run in this order. Each one can run on its own with the command shown, once the stages before it have run. All stages share the modules for the grid, raster input and output and parallel tiles, grid.py, rio.py and pool.py.

fetch

catalog.py, fetch.py · esa fetch wood

Downloads the inputs of the service into the store, the folder that all services share.

Land cover. fetch_clc downloads the Corine accounting layers of version 21 for the five years from the record of each year in the datashare of the European Environment Agency.

Regions. fetch_gisco downloads the NUTS 2024 file at 1:1 million in EPSG:3035 from GISCO. It removes the French outermost regions (FRY) and Svalbard (NO0B2), with their parts in the regions above them, and repairs the five German regions whose boundaries cross themselves.

Annual productivity. fetch_gdmp reads the 36 ten-day files of each year from the S3 interface of the Copernicus Data Space Ecosystem, over the window from 32° W to 45° E and from 27° N to 72° N. It applies the scale and offset of each file, multiplies each value by the number of days of its period and adds the 36 periods up. Reading the 180 files takes several hours, and it needs a free account of the Copernicus Data Space Ecosystem with S3 keys in CDSE_S3_ACCESS_KEY and CDSE_S3_SECRET_KEY.

Carbon budget model. fetch_http downloads the workbook of the EFA FRA CBM data analysis from the open data server of the JRC.

The fetchers write each file under a temporary name and rename it when it is complete, so an interrupted download leaves nothing half written, and esa inputs lists every input with its state. The Eurostat extracts come from the SDMX interface of Eurostat, and the fetcher records the date of retrieval of each one in extracts.json beside it, since a new release can revise past values. The report of each run lists the inputs with those dates.

prepare

inputs.py, forestmaps.py · esa run wood -s prepare

Computes the spatial key, forest times productivity at 100 m, in tiles, and adds it up by country and by 1 km cell. It keeps the resampled productivity of the forest cells in work/shared/forest_gdmp_100m/, which the 100 m maps of wood and carbon read back. It then shares the weight of each country among its NUTS2 and NUTS3 regions and reads the increment of the carbon budget model from its workbook.

accounts

tables.py, accounts.py, workbook.py · esa run wood -s accounts

Fills the series of the net annual increment and of removals, compares them to find overuse and the sustainable flow, screens and fills the unit values, values the accounts in current and 2010 prices and writes the workbook by NUTS level.

maps_1km

maps.py · esa run wood -s maps_1km

Spreads the national flow and its value over the 1 km key and checks the maps, read back from disk, against the tables.

maps_100m

maps.py, forestmaps.py · esa run wood -s maps_100m

Reads the kept productivity in each tile and writes six whole-number maps per year, in cubic decimetres and euro cents, and checks every country against its national values.
