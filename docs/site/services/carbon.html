Carbon sequestration

Method as implemented, and the code of each step

This page describes the method of the carbon sequestration accounts as the tool implements it. Each step points to the stage of the tool that carries it out, and the code annex at the end describes each stage with links to its code. The numbers and tables that a run produces are in the report of that run, in outputs/carbon/report/, which carries the same method text together with them.

1 Methods

1.1 Overview

The accounts deliver five quantities for every reporting year. The flow without offset is the CO2 that land removes from the atmosphere. The actual flow is what remains of those removals once the emissions of land are compensated, and it is the service that reaches society. The missed flow is the difference between the two, which is the part of the removals used up by emissions. The actual flow and the missed flow are also valued in euro. Each quantity is reported in two forms, as tables by country (NUTS0) and by NUTS2 and NUTS3 region in an Excel workbook, and as maps on a grid of 100 m cells. The section on products at the end describes both.

National greenhouse gas inventories report CO2 by country and land category, and they do not say where on the land it is taken up. The work therefore runs in four steps, which the sections below follow in order.

  1. The inventory gives the removals and the emissions of each land category in each country and year.
  2. The offset nets all emissions against all removals across the European Union, and the result is shared among countries. This gives the actual flow, the flow without offset and the missed flow by country.
  3. A price per tonne of CO2 turns the flows into values, in current prices and in constant prices of 2010.
  4. A spatial key, which combines where forest grows with how productive the vegetation is, shares each national value among the regions and the 100 m cells of the country. Regions and cells therefore add up to the national values, and they show where within a country the service is most likely to occur.

The analysis covers the EU27, by country, NUTS2 region and NUTS3 region under the NUTS 2024 boundaries (Eurostat GISCO, 2024). The boundaries carry the notice © EuroGeographics for the administrative boundaries. All grids use the projection EPSG:3035, in which every cell covers the same area on the ground (see the stage prepare in the code annex).

1.2 Removals and emissions

We took CO2 by source sector from the Eurostat dataset env_air_gge (Eurostat, 2026b) for the 27 Member States and the EU27 aggregate (see the stage accounts in the code annex). This dataset carries the national inventories that countries report under the climate conventions, and its part on land use, land use change and forestry (LULUCF) gives the CO2 that each land category exchanges with the atmosphere. Following Vallecillo et al. (2019), the land categories are forest land, cropland, grassland, wetlands, settlements and other land, which are the codes CRF4A to CRF4F.

The inventory uses the point of view of the atmosphere, so a negative value is CO2 removed and a positive value is CO2 released. The accounts reverse the sign, so that a positive balance \(b\) of category \(k\) in country \(c\) is uptake. The removals \(R\) and emissions \(E\) are the two parts of that balance.

\[ R_{k,c} = \max(b_{k,c},\, 0), \qquad E_{k,c} = \max(-b_{k,c},\, 0) \tag{1}\]

A category that is a net source in a year therefore counts fully as an emission and adds nothing to the removals of that year. Forest land can be a net source in a country. A category that a country does not report is set to zero and listed. The residual category CRF4H stays outside the account.

1.2.1 Harvested wood products

The inventory reports the change in the stock of harvested wood products as its own category, CRF4G. A deduction of that sink from the removals of forest land is one possible treatment, since the carbon in harvested wood has left the forest. The carbon chapters of Vallecillo et al. (2019) and La Notte et al. (2021) take forest uptake from the inventory as reported and make no such deduction, and the handbook of Eurostat keeps these products out of net sequestration as a separate category (Eurostat, 2026a, para. 9.56). A decision was therefore made not to deduct them. The sink stays outside the flow as a memo item.

1.3 The offset and the actual flow

La Notte et al. (2021) define the service that reaches society as the balance of removals and emissions over all ecosystem types at the level of the European Union, so an ecosystem type with more emissions than removals provides no service. The EU net flow \(N\) is computed once, over all land categories and countries.

\[ N = \sum_{k,c} R_{k,c} - \sum_{k,c} E_{k,c} \tag{2}\]

La Notte et al. (2021) then attribute this net flow to each country \(c\) in proportion to its removals of forest land \(R_{F,c}\), in their Equation 6.1. This share is the allocation key of the actual flow.

\[ F_c = N \, \frac{R_{F,c}}{\sum_{c} R_{F,c}} \tag{3}\]

The flow without offset \(W_c\) of a country is the sum of its removals over the six land categories, and the missed flow \(M_c\) is the difference between that and the actual flow.

\[ W_c = \sum_{k} R_{k,c}, \qquad M_c = W_c - F_c \tag{4}\]

At the level of the European Union, the missed flow equals total emissions by construction. The definition follows from the distinction that La Notte et al. (2021) draw between the role of forests, shown by removals, and the contribution to society, shown by the actual flow (see the stage accounts in the code annex).

1.3.1 Choice of the allocation key

The allocation key of Equation 3 is a choice, and a natural alternative exists. Net annual increment is the growth of living trees minus natural mortality, so it measures forest growth directly and does not depend on the sign that a category takes in the inventory. Every run compares the two keys. The compared key shares the EU net flow by the net annual increment of all forest from Eurostat (Eurostat, 2026d), with gaps filled from the nearest years of each country, and it also deducts the sink of harvested wood products from the removals of forest land. It assigns a flow to countries whose forest land is a net source and it can leave countries with a negative missed flow which has no direct reading as a service that is not delivered. The series of net annual increment also carries breaks between years, and very few countries have reported it for 2024, so most of the key of that year repeats earlier values.

A decision was made to follow Equation 6.1. Under this key no country has a negative missed flow, and a country whose forest land is a net source receives no flow, which agrees with the definition of the service.

1.3.2 Allocation to emitting sectors

Vallecillo et al. (2019) complement the accounts with a table that attributes the actual flow of each country to the economic sectors in proportion to their share of its total CO2 emissions, which shows which sectors the service offsets. The attribution does not change the flow, and it appears in the report of each run only. We grouped the source categories of the inventory into energy supply, manufacturing and construction, transport, agriculture, forestry and fishing, households, services and other, and waste. These keep the sector names of the inventory, which differ from the industries of the national accounts, as the fuel that households burn in their cars is reported under transport.

1.4 Monetary valuation

The value of a flow is the flow in tonnes of CO2 multiplied by a price per tonne. The accounts need a price for the damage avoided by a tonne of CO2 that stays out of the atmosphere. Vallecillo et al. (2019) use the benchmark of EUR 30 per tonne of CO2 from OECD (2016), which they describe as a reference point and as a lower-end estimate. A decision was made to use a social cost of carbon instead, which is the value of the damage that one additional tonne causes, because it reflects the long-term damage of climate change and does not depend on the conditions of a carbon market in a given year.

The value draws on the meta-analysis of published estimates by Tol (2023). That work standardises every estimate to 2010 US dollars per tonne of carbon, for emissions in 2010 (Tol, 2022), and its update finds a weighted mean of 207 US$ per tonne of carbon, which rises to between 231 and 253 US$ when older studies are discounted (Tol, 2025). A decision was made to adopt 266 US$ per tonne of carbon in 2010 prices, which lies just above that range and well below the kernel mean of 509 US$ in Tol (2022), whose long right tail reflects the large uncertainty of these estimates. We moved it to 2023 prices with the consumer price index of the United States (U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, 2023), a factor of 304.70/218.06, and to euros at the rate of 1.0813 US$ per euro in 2023 (European Central Bank, 2023). Those steps lead to about EUR 344, and the accounts use EUR 343.6 per tonne of carbon. One tonne of CO2 holds 12/44 of a tonne of carbon, so the price per tonne of CO2 is 12/44 of that value in 2023 euros (see the stage prepare in the code annex).

The social cost of carbon is a single real value, so we held it constant in 2023 euros and moved it to the prices of each year \(t\) with the harmonised index of consumer prices \(H\) of the European Union (Eurostat, 2026c). This gives the value in current prices. Constant prices of 2010 then remove general inflation, so that values of different years can be compared (see the stage accounts in the code annex).

\[ P_t = P_{2023}\, \frac{H_t}{H_{2023}}, \qquad V^{\text{current}}_{c,t} = F_{c,t}\, P_t, \qquad V^{\text{constant}}_{c,t} = V^{\text{current}}_{c,t}\, \frac{H_{2010}}{H_t} \tag{5}\]

The missed flow is valued in the same way. The handbook of Eurostat does not value this service (Eurostat, 2026a).

1.5 The spatial key

The steps so far give national values. Regions and map cells receive a part of them through a spatial key, which gives each place a weight, and each place takes the national value times its share of the weight of its country.

\[ F_i = F_c\, \frac{w_i}{\sum_{j \in c} w_j} \tag{6}\]

Because the shares of a country add up to one, regions and cells always add up to the national value, and the key decides only how that value is distributed within the country.

Vallecillo et al. (2019) map forest land only, since it is the one ecosystem type for which almost all countries report uptake, and they spread the national values with dry matter productivity over the woodland and forest of Corine Land Cover. We followed that approach, with the two more recent products that the Earth observation roadmap of the same programme names (Zurbarán Nucci et al., 2025). The weight of a place is therefore the forest on it times the productivity of its vegetation.

Forest comes from the Corine accounting layers, which the European Environment Agency prepares at 100 m for comparison between years, in version 21 for all five years (European Environment Agency, 2026). The classes are broad-leaved forest (311), coniferous forest (312), mixed forest (313) and transitional woodland and shrub (324).

Productivity is Gross Dry Matter Productivity (GDMP), the rate at which vegetation produces dry matter, estimated from satellite images by the Copernicus Land Monitoring Service. The annual value is the sum of the 36 ten-day values of the year, each multiplied by its number of days. The 1 km product, version 2, covers 2000, 2006 and 2012 (Copernicus Land Monitoring Service, n.d.-a), and it ends in 2020. The 300 m product, version 2, covers 2018 and 2024 (Copernicus Land Monitoring Service, n.d.-b), and it starts in 2014. Since 2018 exists in both, each run compares them over the cells of 1 km that are mostly forest. A difference in level between the products changes no national total, because the key only shares a national value within a country (see the stage prepare in the code annex).

The key takes two forms, which share these ingredients and differ in resolution.

  • The 1 km key gives the regional tables. Its forest term \(S_i\) is the share of the 100 m cells of each 1 km cell that are forest, and its productivity \(G_i\) is the productivity on the 1 km grid, the mean of the 300 m values within each kilometre in 2018 and 2024 and the 1 km product interpolated bilinearly to the grid in the earlier years. The weight is \(w_i = S_i\, G_i\).
  • The 100 m key gives the maps. Its forest term is 1 for a 100 m cell of forest and 0 otherwise, and productivity is interpolated bilinearly to the 100 m grid.

Both forms place the same national totals. Within a country they agree closely, but they interpolate productivity differently, so the 100 m maps added up over a region can differ slightly from the value of that region in the tables.

1.6 Regional values

NUTS2 and NUTS3 regions receive a share of the national values (see the stage prepare in the code annex). The actual flow of a region is the national flow times the share of the region in the 1 km key of its country. The flow without offset adds up removals of six land categories, and only one of them is forest, so each category is shared in proportion to the area of that category in the region, according to the Corine accounting layer of the year. The missed flow of a region is the difference between the two. When a country has no mapped area of a category, the share of the region in the total mapped land of the country stands in.

1.7 Products

Workbook. The workbook holds one sheet for each quantity and NUTS level. The sheets of the flow without offset, the actual flow and the missed flow give thousand tonnes of CO2 and of carbon, and those of the value of the actual and the missed flow give million euro in current prices and in constant prices of 2010. The reporting years are the columns, and a first sheet explains the contents.

Maps. There are five maps for each reporting year on the 100 m grid, built with the 100 m key (see the stage maps_100m in the code annex). The first is the actual flow and the second its value at constant 2010 prices. The third is the flow without offset, for which a decision was made to map the removals of forest land, following Vallecillo et al. (2019), because other land categories switch between source and sink across years and countries and the key places forest only. This map therefore holds less than the flow without offset in the tables, which report the difference by region. The fourth and fifth are the missed flow and its value. The missed flow is a balance between removals and emissions and has no place of its own on the land, so a decision was made to allocate the national value with the key of the actual flow. These two maps show where the removals that compensate emissions are attributed, and they do not show where any loss of the service occurs.

Each cell holds a whole number in small units, kilograms of CO2 or euro cents at 2010 prices. Whole numbers take about a third of the disk of decimal numbers, and every country keeps its total to within one part in a thousand. The files are Cloud Optimised GeoTIFF without overviews, which keeps them small, and a GIS such as QGIS draws them quickly as they are.

Working layers. The run also spreads the same national values over the 1 km key. These 1 km layers are not a product of the accounts. They give the map figures of the report, drawn at 10 km, and the checks of the 1 km key against the tables (see the stage maps_1km in the code annex).

1.8 Checks

The run stops if any check fails. It compares the sectors with total emissions without land use, and the total of land use, CRF4, with the sum of its parts.

The 1 km layers are read back from disk and compared with the tables, and the 100 m maps are added up by country from the whole numbers as they are written and compared with the national values. The run also counts the countries that hold a flow and lack forest cells, which would lose that flow on the map.

Code annex

The stages run in this order. Each one can run on its own with the command shown, once the stages before it have run. All stages share the modules for the grid, raster input and output and parallel tiles, grid.py, rio.py and pool.py.

fetch

catalog.py, fetch.py · esa fetch carbon

Downloads the inputs of the service into the store, the folder that all services share.

Land cover. fetch_clc downloads the Corine accounting layers of version 21 for the five years from the record of each year in the datashare of the European Environment Agency.

Regions. fetch_gisco downloads the NUTS 2024 file at 1:1 million in EPSG:3035 from GISCO. It removes the French outermost regions (FRY) and Svalbard (NO0B2), with their parts in the regions above them, and repairs the five German regions whose boundaries cross themselves.

Annual productivity. fetch_gdmp reads the 36 ten-day files of each year from the S3 interface of the Copernicus Data Space Ecosystem, over the window from 32° W to 45° E and from 27° N to 72° N. It applies the scale and offset of each file, multiplies each value by the number of days of its period and adds the 36 periods up. Reading the 180 files takes several hours, and it needs a free account of the Copernicus Data Space Ecosystem with S3 keys in CDSE_S3_ACCESS_KEY and CDSE_S3_SECRET_KEY.

The fetchers write each file under a temporary name and rename it when it is complete, so an interrupted download leaves nothing half written, and esa inputs lists every input with its state. The Eurostat extracts come from the SDMX interface of Eurostat, and the fetcher records the date of retrieval of each one in extracts.json beside it, since a new release can revise past values. The report of each run lists the inputs with those dates.

prepare

inputs.py, landcover.py · esa run carbon -s prepare

Reduces Corine to the share of each 1 km cell in each land category of the inventory, moves the annual productivity to the 1 km grid, adds land and forest weight up by region and builds the carbon price from the social cost of carbon and the price index.

accounts

tables.py, accounts.py, workbook.py · esa run carbon -s accounts

Splits the inventory into removals and emissions, computes the EU offset and the actual, offset-free and missed flows, allocates the flow to emitting sectors, values it and shares it among regions, and writes the workbook.

maps_1km

maps.py · esa run carbon -s maps_1km

Spreads the national values over forest share times productivity at 1 km, writes five maps per year and checks them against the tables.

maps_100m

maps.py, forestmaps.py · esa run carbon -s maps_100m

Computes the 100 m key in tiles from the productivity kept in work/shared/forest_gdmp_100m/ (or resamples it and keeps it, when wood has not run), writes five whole-number maps per year, in kilograms of CO2 and euro cents, and checks every country against its national values.
